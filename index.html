The difference is in the details.

A top that means it

1/4", 3/8" or 1/2" steel plate, laser-cut with 5/8" fixture holes on a precise 2" grid (or 16 mm on 50 mm, if that's your tooling). Flat where it counts, with clamping anywhere on the surface, and enough mass to shrug off years of grinding and hammer work.

Laser-cut plate top

Clamp from every edge

Perforated side skirts wrap the full perimeter, so fixture blocks, stops and clamps work on all four sides, not just the top. Square once, clamp everywhere, weld with both hands free.

Full-perimeter hole grid

Rigid by design

An interlocking ribbed substructure ties the top to heavy tube legs, resisting twist, sag and drum. It goes together square and it stays that way, on casters or planted on fixed feet. Every table carries a rated load worked out from its own plate and ribs.

Ribbed substructure

If you can run a bead, you can build it. Parts are laser-cut with interlocking tabs and slots that self-fixture the structure square, and every table ships with a step-by-step assembly guide with a picture for each step. Most customers have a table standing in an afternoon, and you'll trust a table you welded yourself.

Files download minutes after checkout. Kits typically cut and ship within 5 to 7 business days. Welded tables run 2 to 3 weeks depending on finish and queue. Powder-coated builds add a few days. You get a tracking number the day it leaves Denmark.

Tables ship LTL freight on a skid, nationwide. If you don't have a forklift or dock, add liftgate delivery at checkout and the driver brings it down to the ground for you.

1/4" is flat, rigid and right for most fab and hobby work. Go 3/8" if you regularly hammer, press or build heavy weldments on your table, or you just want the extra mass in reserve. 1/2" is for the biggest frames and the hardest use. The designer shows the rated load for each.
